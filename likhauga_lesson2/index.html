Meaning-making · 7 minutes

Identity is not only inherited. It is constructed.

Inherited

Language, ancestry, place, stories, and traditions are received from others.

Practiced

Identity becomes real through making, performing, gathering, and remembering.

Expressed

People communicate belonging, beliefs, roles, and values through artistic choices.

Negotiated

Communities preserve, adapt, combine, or challenge traditions as life changes.

Identity is a living process: who we receive + who we practice + who we choose to become.
Meaning-making · 8 minutes

Worldview appears as recurring relationships.

Place + knowledge
Community + participation
Belief + ritual
Memory + story
Land & natureMaterials, livelihood, seasons, waters, and place-based knowledge.
Kapwa & communityShared work, kinship, belonging, and participation.
Spirit & meaningSacred life, ritual, ancestors, protection, and the unseen.
ContinuityKnowledge carried through elders, practice, memory, and story.
Exchange & changeTrade, migration, contact, colonization, adaptation, and technology.
Dignity & identityA people’s name, voice, history, skill, and right to self-representation.
These are possible signals—not traits shared identically by every Filipino or every cultural community.
A practical analysis tool · Use the photographs as evidence

Enter through five evidence doors.

01 · FORM

What?

Sound, shape, rhythm, movement, color, structure.

02 · MATERIAL

From what?

Abaca, wood, bamboo, brass, voice, body.

03 · SYMBOL

Meaning?

Motif, gesture, character, story, image.

04 · FUNCTION

Why?

Ritual, work, celebration, teaching, status.

05 · TRANSMISSION

How carried?

Family, elder, master, practice, community.

Strong interpretation = observation + context + respectful evidence.

Application · Activity 4: Tradition in Motion

Adapt with respect—not erasure.

Challenge: Propose a contemporary presentation of one living art. Your plan must preserve essential meaning, name the source community, and show what requires consultation or consent.

Complete all five decisions.

1Living art: choose a tradition still practiced, taught, performed, or made today.
2New setting: identify where and for whom it will be presented.
3Must remain: protect its community name, core meaning, source, or essential process.
4May change carefully: adjust only format, length, technology, or staging—with guidance.
5Promise: state how you will consult, ask permission, credit, and share benefits.
DESIGN15:00
A living tradition, responsibly presented.

Your choices will build the concept below.

Choose one tradition that a community continues to practice or transmit.
Name the audience and why this setting helps learning—not just entertainment.
Protect what makes the tradition culturally meaningful.
Do not change sacred or restricted elements without community guidance.
A strong promise identifies who will be consulted and what respectful action will be taken.
Use the completed PNG during the 45-second curator pitch.
Values integration · Activity 5: Culture Keeper Pledge

Cultural respect begins with how we represent others.

A tradition is not merely content to borrow. It carries people, memory, knowledge, and dignity.
Type a personal 2–4 sentence pledge:
Before using it:
learn the context, identify the community, and ask what is restricted.
While presenting it:
credit sources and culture bearers; avoid stereotypes and false claims.
When adapting it:
consult, seek permission when needed, and let community voices lead.
The button requires a meaningful typed response.

Core values: respect, responsibility, humility, and stewardship.
